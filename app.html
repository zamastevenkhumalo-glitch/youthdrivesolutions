📘 Things to Know

A quick study guide before you begin

PART 1 — About Your Learner's Licence

What it is: A learner's licence is a permit that allows you to drive on public roads only while a fully licensed driver sits beside you. It proves you understand the road signs, rules and how a vehicle works.

Why you need it: It is a legal requirement before you can book the practical driver's test at any DLTC.

Minimum age: 17 years for a Code B (light motor vehicle) learner's licence.

How long it lasts: 24 months from the date of issue. If you don't pass your driver's test in that time you must write the learner's test again.

PART 2 — Documents & Licensing Categories

Documents you need: Original ID or valid passport, proof of residential address (utility bill), two identical ID photos. If you are under 18, a parent or guardian must give written consent.

Categories of learner's licence: Code A1 (light motorcycles up to 125cc), Code A (motorcycles over 125cc), Code B (cars and light bakkies up to 3,500kg), Code C1 (medium heavy vehicles up to 16,000kg), Code EC (heavy articulated trucks over 16,000kg).

PART 3 — What the Practical Test Looks Like

Yard test (about 20 minutes): The examiner asks you to do a pre-trip inspection, start the vehicle, and complete three manoeuvres — alley docking, parallel parking, and an incline start. If you fail here, you cannot proceed to the road test.

Road test (30–45 minutes): You drive on public roads with the examiner beside you. They watch how you observe (mirror, blind spot, signal, action), how you handle intersections, turn, park and follow the rules of the road.

PART 4 — The Golden Habit: Observation

Every time you turn, change lane, park, or move off, follow this order:

1. Rear-view mirror
2. Relevant side mirror
3. Blind spot (over your shoulder)
4. Signal
5. Blind spot again
6. Only then take action

Never signal before checking mirrors — the examiner will mark you down heavily.

PART 5 — Instant-Fail Actions to Avoid

Not checking your blind spot before moving off, turning or changing lanes. Rolling backwards on a hill start. Not stopping completely at a STOP sign. Running a red light. Mounting a kerb. Not wearing your seatbelt. Speeding (urban 60, rural/open 100, freeway 120, school zones 40). Any dangerous action forcing other traffic to brake or swerve.

PART 6 — How to Use This App

Each topic — Rules of the Road, Signs of the Road, and Controlling the Vehicle — is broken into three parts: A, B and C. Each part has 30 questions. Finish all three parts of a topic to complete it.

You must score 80% or more to pass each part. Wrong answers are shown at the end so you can learn from them.

Take your time. Study well. Good luck. 🚗
